# Coach's read — Cascais, 9 September 2026

Relayed with an annotated replay of race 1. The replay's own wind label reads **317°T**,
which is exactly the first-beat figure in the event report, so the picture and the data
are describing the same moment.

## The mechanism

The breeze **bends against the shoreline**. That is what makes the right side mostly the
stronger one.

This is the same effect as the GRIB error. The models sit **+18.6° to the right** of the
wind the fleet actually races in because a 7 km grid cell cannot resolve a bend that tight
against the coast. "Right is stronger" and "the model is wrong" are one finding seen from
two directions — and it explains why the committee's line kept coming out pin-biased:
the RC squares to something close to the model.

## The start — the biggest decision in the race

- **Right is stronger, but do not pay for it.** Never sacrifice 100–200 m of line bias to
  get to the right. A committee-boat start cannot win that back.
- **If you can see bias, take it.** If the bias is visible at all it is probably big enough
  to be worth more than anything else available on the first beat.
- **The pin is a long way from the course.** The top mark sat above the RC boat, so the pin
  was about 1 km across the course axis. Computed from the logged line ends and the fleet's
  own beat bearing: **1004 m** in race 1 and **1000 m** in race 2. Starting there only works
  if you can go right almost immediately.
- **Nothing on the beat gives it back.** Spend the pre-start gathering clues, not settling.

### What the telemetry says about that trade

| Race | Line | Bias at the gun | Pin offset from the course axis | Who reached the mark first |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1004 m | 6° = 107 m, pin | 1004 m | pin end (r = +0.55) |
| 2 | 1006 m | 12° = 206 m, pin | 1000 m | pin end (r = +0.36) |

The bias won in both races despite the displacement — boats starting nearer the committee
boat took measurably longer up the first beat. So the rule "take the bias, then get right"
is what the data supports, in that order.

## Gate bias

Gate bias mattered as much as the line. With the left shift at the bottom of the course,
the **right-hand gate turn came out considerably further upwind**.

Not yet computed from telemetry — see the open item on downwind-gate choice in
`ANALYSIS-BRIEF.md`. The gate positions and each boat's chosen side are both recoverable
from the logs, so this is measurable rather than merely observed.

## Top mark

A **gybe set** was the right call — but not from the front pack, where the offset boats
throw a lot of dirty air. Spread fleet and more breeze makes it easy; compact fleet and
less breeze makes the wind shadow big enough to change the answer.

## Between races

Sail between races. Check wind direction, line bias and top-mark position every time. The
biggest decision of the next race is made before the gun, not after it.
