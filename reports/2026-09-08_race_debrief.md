**The boat is fast enough. The losses are navigational.**

## The start-line pattern — consistent across both races

Correlation between where a boat started along the line (0 = pin, 1 = committee boat) and
its time to the first windward mark:

- Race 1: **+0.55**
- Race 2: **+0.36**

Both positive: **boats starting nearer the committee-boat end took longer to the windward
mark, in both races.** The podium supports it —

- Race 1 top three started at 0.28, 0.45, 0.65
- Race 2 top four started at 0.47, 0.26, 0.36, 0.08

The team started at **0.45 in Race 1 (3rd at the mark)** and **0.93 in Race 2 (11th)**.
The other boat-end starter in Race 2, J70 Let it be at 0.91, finished the beat 12th of 12.

**Recommendation: start mid-line to pin.** On this course configuration the boat end was
penalised in both races. This is the single clearest actionable finding in the data.

## What this data still cannot tell you

- **Actual finishing positions and points.** RACE_END is a synced signal — every boat's
  lands within a second of every other's — so it is not a finish time. All positions above
  are at mark roundings, among the 13 tracked boats only, not the full 103-boat fleet.
- **Wind speed.** No boat logged a wind instrument. Direction is estimated from geometry;
  speed is unavailable by any method here.
- **Why** the extra distance was sailed — whether it was a tactical call, traffic, or being
  forced onto a bad lane off the start. The track shows the cost, not the intent.
- **Downwind gybe counts** are unreliable: the manoeuvre detector keys on speed dips and
  over-counts when the boat is surfing.
